## Why CADC+

So basically our goal is to validate is any of the methods/techniques proposed on these papers actually help in snow, so currently when they evaluated these techniques they were evaluated in different location in snow and without snow conditions or the simulated snow/no snow conditions to keep the location same. But in case one, we don't know if the gains or improvments we are seeing are either because of location/time/weather change or just the snow/no snow thing, there are too many variables moving. Meanwhile in second kind of evaluations the location is kept fixed but the simulated conditions are not 100% accurate as they would have been naturally, for example the synthetic disturbance that should be there in lidar data while snowing/fog is there, but what about changes on the ground?? accumulated snow like vehicles covered in snow and are not visible clearly etc. Meanwhile, CADC+ try to solve this problem, it contains natural photage of same location on different days around similar time or closes conditions on a snow and no snow day, so only one variable (close to one) is changing, so the evaluations will be more honest and we might be able to narrow down if the gains are actually because of the techniques or just artifacts because of other variables changing.

## Three Predictions

1. Weather-only robustness (Level 1→2). We expect UMT to shrink the AP drop the most of the three, because it's the only arm that trains on signal from real CADC snow (via unlabeled pseudo-labeling), even though it never sees ground-truth labels there — its uncertainty-weighted mean-teacher setup was validated specifically on real cross-weather targets in its own paper, so this is the closest match between what it was designed for and what Level 2 asks of it. ETH-sim should also reduce the drop relative to baseline, but by less than UMT — it never sees real snow at all, only physically-simulated snow injected into clear scans, and even after recalibrating its beam model to CADC's 32-beam sensor, it can't reproduce effects like accumulated snow on the ground or partial vehicle occlusion that CADC+'s real Level 2 data actually contains. Robo3D's density-insensitive training should help least of the three, since it targets generic point-density robustness (motion blur, beam dropout, sensor failure) rather than anything snow-specific, so any benefit here would be incidental rather than targeted. The plain baseline should show the largest drop, having no weather handling at all.

2. The location-shift ceiling (Level 2→3). We predict the added degradation here will be similar across all four arms because none of the three strategies was built to address anything about where the car is driving — UMT adapts to CADC's own snowy sequences, ETH-sim only perturbs point clouds sampled from CADC-clear, and Robo3D's training never sees geography at all. All three interventions operate entirely on sensor-level appearance (noise, dropout, simulated particles), not on scene-level distribution (unfamiliar road layouts, traffic density, object frequency), so once you move to unmatched routes, that shift should land on every arm roughly equally — the weather-robustness intervention and the location-shift problem are orthogonal, and none of these methods was designed to touch the second one at all.

3. The budget/null hypothesis. If no strategy beats the matched-budget baseline at Level 2, that wouldn't mean the three papers are wrong about their own mechanisms — uncertainty-weighted pseudo-labeling, physical snow modeling, and density-insensitive training are all reasonable ideas in principle. It would mean that a meaningful chunk of each paper's reported gain likely comes from comparing against a baseline that had less effective training exposure, or from evaluating on synthetic corruptions that don't capture real snow's actual failure modes — rather than from the specific mechanism each paper credits. Practically, that tells a team: don't adopt the added engineering complexity of any of these three methods on the strength of the original papers' numbers alone, because under a fair, real-data, budget-matched comparison the advantage may not survive.
